# Something that happens to a character: lost time

A proposal (2026-11-21), not built. It follows `performance-model.md` and the critique of Bellona act 1: the play establishes Kidd's fear of losing time and never shows it happen, because no party can make it happen. The director settles facts before the play; the planner may not decide what becomes of a body; an actor cannot lose its own hours.

## What is wanted

Once, early, the audience sees the rule: lights down on Kidd in one place, lights up on him somewhere else, later, with no idea what he did. After that, Lanya's pressing him is about evidence and not curiosity.

## The event

A **gap** is something the world does to one character, when the director calls for it.

| Step | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| The play says who can lose time | The play file | Kidd's description already does ("you lose time, minutes or hours at a stretch, and come back somewhere else"). A play marks the character so that the engine knows: nothing new is stated about him |
| Whether, and when | The director | At a scene's end, at most once in an act, and only for a character so marked. It gives no reason and decides nothing of what follows |
| What happened in it | The director, in one sentence, from what lies behind the play | What Kidd did in the missing time, as someone watching would have seen it. It is a fact of the play, known at first only to whoever was there |
| Carrying it out | The world | The clock moves on by the length of the gap. Kidd is at a place the director named, among those the world lists. What the sentence says he did to the world is done by the resolver, as any deed is (a page written, a thing taken, a mark left) |
| What each then has | The record | Those who were with him and could see have the sentence, as something they saw. Kidd has nothing for those minutes: his last memory is the moment before, and he is told only where and when he finds himself and what is in his hands |
| The page | | One line for the lights going down, and the next scene's set-up opens where he comes to |

What follows is the actors' own. Kidd may hide it, ask, or guess; Lanya may tell him what she saw, or not.

## Why this shape

- **It keeps the rule that everyone proposes and only the world disposes.** The director calls the gap and says what happened in it; the world checks and applies it. Nobody decides what Kidd makes of it, or whether Lanya tells.
- **It is the only thing an actor cannot refuse.** That is right for an affliction, and it is why it must be rare, marked in the play file, and never used for anything a character could simply choose to do.
- **What he did is settled, not left blank.** A gap in which nothing is known to anyone gives the other characters nothing to hold, and the audience nothing to learn later. One sentence, seen by whoever was there, is enough to turn Lanya's questions into testimony.
- **It is general.** The same event serves a faint, a seizure, sleepwalking, a drug taking hold: a stretch in which a character acts and does not know it. Only the play's own words say which a character has.

## What it needs

- A mark on a character in a play file, and a place for the director's call in its reading between scenes (`director.read`).
- One new event of the world, with the clock, the move and the resolver's effects inside it, and record entries that leave the one it happened to out of their witnesses.
- A line for the set-up check, so that the next scene opens from where and when he comes to.
- Whole-prompt reading and a replay for the director's prompt, on Bellona's act 1.

## Questions for Bruce

1. **Is what he did settled** (one sentence from the director, seen by those present), as proposed; or should it stay unknown to everyone, with only its traces in the world?
2. **Who was there.** If Lanya is with him when it starts, does she go along (and see it), or is she left where she stood, to find him afterwards? The first gives her testimony; the second gives her a search.
3. **With a danger.** The critique suggests tying the gap to the gang: he loses time during an encounter and comes back with no memory of it. That would be the director calling a gap while a danger is on the stage, and saying in its sentence what he did about it. Allowed?
4. **How often.** Once an act at most is proposed. Should the first act always have one, so the rule is shown early?
